1.5. Primary Decomposition. A ring1 is Noetherian if every ascending chain of ideals
eventually stabilizes. Note that every quotient ring of a Noetherian ring is Noetherian.
A topological space is Noetherian if every descending chain of closed subsets even-
tually stabilizes. Note that every closed subset of a Noetherian space is Noetherian.

Proposition 1.38. Let X be a Noetherian space. Then every open subset of X is quasi-compact.2
Proof. Let U := {Uλ}λ∈Λ be an open cover of X. Consider the set of finite unions of
members of U . This set has a maximal member. Indeed, otherwise, there is an infinite
ascending chain of open sets, hence an infinite descending chain of closed sets. The
corresponding members of U form a finite subcover of X.
□

Theorem 1.43 (Lasker–Noether). Every proper ideal in a Noetherian ring is a finite inter-
section of primary ideals.
Proof. In view of Lemma 1.42, it is enough to show that every proper ideal of a
Noetherian ring R is a finite intersection of irreducible ideals.
If not, Proposition
1.36 gives a maximal member J among the family of ideals of R which are not finite
intersections of irreducible ideals. It is not irreducible, so J = J1 ∩J2 with J ⊊J1, J2. By
maximality, J1 and J2 are finite intersections of irreducible ideals, and hence, does the
same for J. We get a contradiction.
□

is called a primary decomposition. This decomposition may not be unique.

2. Functions on Affine Varieties
2.1. Regular functions. For a ring R, an R-algebra is a ring A equipped with a ring
homomorphism φ: R →A. A subalgebra of A is a subring S such that φ(R) ⊆S. An
R-algebras homomorphism f : A →B is a ring homomorphism between R-algebras
A and B such that f(ra) = rf(a) for all r ∈R and a ∈A. If furthermore f is bijective, we
call f an isomorphism and we write A  B. In this case, f −1 is also an isomorphism.
An R-algebra A is said to be finitely generated over R if there is a surjective R-algebra
homomorphism f : R[x1, . . . , xn] →A. In this case, A is generated by f(x1), . . . , f(xn).
Let X ⊆An be an affine algebraic set. Its coordinate ring is
A(X) := C[x1, . . . , xn]/I(X)
which is a finitely generated C-algebra. Proposition 1.5 says that X is an affine variety
if and only if A(X) is an integral domain. The class [ f] ∈A(X) defines the polynomial
function X →C given by p 7→f(p). This is independent of the representative. We
usually write f for both a class and its polynomial function. For an ideal I ⊆A(X),
there is an ideal J ⊆C[x1, . . . , xn] with J/I(X) = I. We define V(I) := V(J) ∩X. By
Corollary 1.35, there is a one-to-one correspondence between primes ideals of A(X)
and irreducible closed subsets of X. For f ∈A(X), the principal open subset DX(f) is
likewise independent of a polynomial representative.
