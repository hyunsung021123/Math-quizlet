2. Functions on Affine Varieties
2.1. Regular functions. For a ring R, an R-algebra is a ring A equipped with a ring
homomorphism φ: R →A. A subalgebra of A is a subring S such that φ(R) ⊆S. An
R-algebras homomorphism f : A →B is a ring homomorphism between R-algebras
A and B such that f(ra) = rf(a) for all r ∈R and a ∈A. If furthermore f is bijective, we
call f an isomorphism and we write A  B. In this case, f −1 is also an isomorphism.
An R-algebra A is said to be finitely generated over R if there is a surjective R-algebra
homomorphism f : R[x1, . . . , xn] →A. In this case, A is generated by f(x1), . . . , f(xn).
Let X ⊆An be an affine algebraic set. Its coordinate ring is
A(X) := C[x1, . . . , xn]/I(X)
which is a finitely generated C-algebra. Proposition 1.5 says that X is an affine variety
if and only if A(X) is an integral domain. The class [ f] ∈A(X) defines the polynomial
function X →C given by p 7→f(p). This is independent of the representative. We
usually write f for both a class and its polynomial function. For an ideal I ⊆A(X),
there is an ideal J ⊆C[x1, . . . , xn] with J/I(X) = I. We define V(I) := V(J) ∩X. By
Corollary 1.35, there is a one-to-one correspondence between primes ideals of A(X)
and irreducible closed subsets of X. For f ∈A(X), the principal open subset DX(f) is
likewise independent of a polynomial representative.

Lemma 2.5. Let X be an affine variety, U ⊆X be a nonempty open subset, and g, h ∈OX(U).
If g = h on a nonempty open subset of U, then g = h on U.
Proof. Let W ⊆U be a nonempty open subset on which g −h = 0, and fix p ∈U. On
some open neighborhood N ⊆U of p, we may write g −h = a/b with a, b ∈A(X) and
b nowhere zero. Note that W ∩N ⊆X is a dense open subset. As a = 0 on W ∩N, we
get a = 0 on X. Thus g(p) = h(p). Since p was arbitrary, g = h on U.
□

Theorem 2.6. We have the following:
(1) For affine varieties X and Y, affine morphisms ϕ: X →Y correspond bijectively to C-
algebra homomorphisms ϕ∗: A(Y) →A(X).
(2) Let ϕ: X →Y and ψ: Y →Z be affine morphisms between affine varieties. Then ψ ◦ϕ is
an affine morphism, and (ψ ◦ϕ)∗= ϕ∗◦ψ∗.
(3) An affine morphism ϕ: X →Y is an isomorphism if and only if ϕ∗is an isomorphism.
(4) Every regular map from an open subset of an affine variety to an affine algebraic set is
continuous.
Proof. Let ϕ: X →Y be an affine morphism with Y ⊆Am whose coordinate classes
are denoted by [y1], . . . , [ym]. We may write ϕ(p) = (f1(p), . . . , fm(p)) for fi ∈A(X). For
G ∈A(Y), define
ϕ∗(G) := G ◦ϕ = [G(f1, . . . , fm)].
It is easy to check that ϕ∗is a C-algebra homomorphism. Conversely, let α: A(Y) →
A(X) be a C-algebra homomorphism. Put fj := α([yj]). For every G ∈I(Y), we have
G(f1, . . . , fm) = α([G]) = 0. As Y = V(I(Y)), we see that (f1(p), . . . , fm(p)) ∈Y for every
p ∈X. Thus we get an affine morphism X →Y given by p 7→( f1(p), . . . , fm(p)). The
coordinate classes [y1], . . . , [ym] generate A(Y) as a C-algebra, so the two constructions
are inverse to each other. This proves (1), and (2), (3) are clear. For (4), let ϕ: U →Y
be a regular map, where U is an open subset of an affine variety X and Y is an affine
algebraic set. For H ∈A(Y), we have ϕ−1(DY(H)) = DX(H ◦ϕ) = DX(ϕ∗(H)). Thus ϕ is
continuous.
□
