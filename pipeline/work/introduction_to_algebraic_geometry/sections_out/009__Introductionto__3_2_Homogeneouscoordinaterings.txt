Proposition 3.8. For every projective algebraic set X, the ideal I(X) is radical.
If X is
nonempty, then
bX = V(I(X)) ⊆AN+1 and I(bX) = I(X).
Furthermore, X is irreducible if and only if I(X) is prime.
Proof. If X = ∅, then I(X) = S. Assume henceforth that X is nonempty. It is clear that
bX = V(I(X)) ⊆AN+1 and I(bX) = I(X). By the strong Nullstellensatz, I(X) is radical. For
the last statement, observe first that if X is reducible, then bX is reducible so that I(X) is
not prime by Proposition 1.5. Conversely, assume that X is irreducible. First, suppose
that FG ∈I(X) for homogeneous F, G ∈S. Then X = (X ∩V+(F)) ∪(X ∩V+(G)), so
X = X ∩V+(F) or X = X ∩V+(G). Thus F ∈I(X) or G ∈I(X). Suppose that FG ∈I(X)
for F, G ∈S. Then the product of the lowest degree homogenous parts of F and G
are contained in i(X). Then the lowest-degree homogeneous part of one of the two is
contained in I(X). By subtracting off this part and repeating the same argument, we
conclude that one of the two is contained in I(X). Hence I(X) is prime.
□

3.3. Quasi-projective varieties. A quasi-projective algebraic set is an open subset
of a projective algebraic set. It is a quasi-projective variety if it is nonempty and
irreducible. An algebraic variety usually means a quasi-projective variety. Let X ⊆PN
be a quasi-projective variety. Then each X ∩Ui can be regarded as an irreducible
open subset of an affine variety, which is covered by open affine subsets. Hence every
quasi-projective variety is covered by open affine subsets.
For an open subset U ⊆X, a function g: U →C is regular at p ∈U if there are a
neighborhood W ⊆U of p and homogeneous polynomials F, G ∈C[x0, . . . , xN] of the
same degree such that G is nowhere zero on W and
g(q) = F(q)
G(q) for q ∈W.
It is regular on U if it is regular at every point of U. The ring of regular functions on
U is denoted by OX(U).
