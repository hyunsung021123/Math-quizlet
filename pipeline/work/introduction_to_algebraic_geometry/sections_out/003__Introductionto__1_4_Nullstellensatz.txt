ideals of Rn and affine algebraic sets in An by I 7→V(I) and X 7→I(X). Furthermore, we have
the following:

(1) Affine varieties in An correspond to prime ideals of Rn.
(2) Points of An correspond to maximal ideals of Rn: the point (a1, . . . , an) corresponds to
ma := (x1 −a1, . . . , xn −an).
Proof. If X is an affine algebraic set in An, then V(I(X)) = X by Proposition 1.4. If I is a
radical ideal of Rn, then the strong Nullstellensatz yields I(V(I)) = I. This proves the
assignments I 7→V(I) and X 7→I(X) give a one-to-one correspondence. The inclusion-
reversing property is guaranteed by Proposition 1.1. Now, for a prime ideal p of Rn,
the weak Nullstellensatz makes V(p) nonempty, and the strong Nullstellensatz implies
I(V(p)) = p. Proposition 1.5 then shows (1). Next, as the kernel of the evaluation map
Rn →C given by f 7→f(a) is ma, we see that ma is maximal. Conversely, suppose
that m is a maximal ideal of Rn. By the weak Nullstellensatz, there is a ∈V(m). Then
m ⊆ma, and maximality forces equality. This proves (2).
□
1.5. Primary Decomposition. A ring1 is Noetherian if every ascending chain of ideals
eventually stabilizes. Note that every quotient ring of a Noetherian ring is Noetherian.
A topological space is Noetherian if every descending chain of closed subsets even-
tually stabilizes. Note that every closed subset of a Noetherian space is Noetherian.
Proposition 1.36. For a ring R, the following are equivalent:
(1) Every ideal of R is finitely generated.
(2) Every ascending chain of ideals of R eventually stabilizes.
(3) Every nonempty family of ideals of R has a maximal member.
Proof. Let I1 ⊆I2 ⊆I3 ⊆· · · be an ascending chain of ideals of R. As S∞
i=1 Ii is an ideal of
R, (1) implies that the finite generators of I lie in one Ik. Then Ik = S∞

all i ≥k. This proves (1) =⇒(2). If a nonempty family of ideals of R has no maximal
member, then this produces a nonstationary ascending chain. Thus (2) =⇒(3). For
any ideal I of R, we can choose a maximal finitely generated subideal J using (3). If
f ∈I, then J + ( f) is again finitely generated and contained in I, so maximality gives
f ∈J. Thus I = J. Hence (3) =⇒(1). The final statements follow from Hilbert basis
theorem and Corollary 1.35.

Example 1.37. By Hilbert basis theorem, Rn and all of its quotient rings are Noetherian.
By Corollary 1.35, An and all of its closed subsets are Noetherian.
Proposition 1.38. Let X be a Noetherian space. Then every open subset of X is quasi-compact.2
Proof. Let U := {Uλ}λ∈Λ be an open cover of X. Consider the set of finite unions of
members of U . This set has a maximal member. Indeed, otherwise, there is an infinite
ascending chain of open sets, hence an infinite descending chain of closed sets. The
corresponding members of U form a finite subcover of X.
□
