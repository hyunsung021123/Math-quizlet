A Gr¨obner basis G of an ideal I ⊆Rn is reduced if every g ∈G is monic and no
monomial of g is divisible by LM≺(h) for any h ∈G \ {g}.
Proposition 1.24. Every nonzero ideal I ⊆Rn has a unique reduced Gr¨obner basis.
Proof. Start with a finite Gr¨obner basis G of I. Delete every element whose leading
monomial is divisible by another leading monomial, until the remaining leading
monomials are the minimal monomial generators of in≺(I). Make the elements monic.
For each g ∈G, replace it by LM≺(g) + RG\{g}(g −LM≺(g)). This replacement remains in
I, and does not change the generated ideal. Minimality of mi ensures that the leading
monomial survives. Thus the resulting set G is a reduced Gr¨obner basis of I. Now,
suppose that G and H are two reduced Gr¨obner bases of I. Note that the minimal
monomial generators of a monomial ideal are unique. Thus, for each g ∈G, there is
a unique h ∈H with the same leading monomial. Since both are monic, the leading
terms cancel in g −h ∈I. Every remaining monomial in g −h is outside in≺(I) by
reducedness. By Corollary 1.20, we get g −h = 0. Repeating the arguments, we finally
obtain G = H.
□

1.3. Elimination. The elimination theorem provides a connection between Gr¨obner
bases and algebraic geometry.
Theorem 1.26 (Elimination Theorem). Use lexicographic order x1 ≻x2 ≻· · · ≻xn on Rn.
Let I ⊆Rn be an ideal. For each 1 ≤j ≤n −1, set
Ij := I ∩C[xj+1, . . . , xn].
If G is a Gr¨obner basis of I, then
Gj := G ∩C[xj+1, . . . , xn]
is a Gr¨obner basis of Ij for every 1 ≤j ≤n −1.
Proof. For f ∈Rn, we have f ∈C[xj+1, . . . , xn] if and only if LM≺(f) ∈C[xj+1, . . . , xn].
Choose 0 , f ∈Ij. Since G is a Gr¨obner basis of I, there is g ∈G such that LM≺(g)
divides LM≺(f). The latter monomial contains none of x1, . . . , xj, so neither does the
former. Thus g ∈Gj. This shows that the leading monomials of Gj generate in≺(Ij).
□
