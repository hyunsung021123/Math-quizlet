Corollary 5.13. Let A be an affine C-domain. Then all maximal strict chains of prime ideals
of A have the same length dim A.
Proof. We proceed by induction on n := dim A. If n = 0, then every prime ideal is
(0) and the assertion is trivial. Assume n ≥1. Let p0 ⊊p1 ⊊· · · ⊊pr be a maximal
chain of prime ideals of A. Note that p0 = (0) and p1 is a minimal prime ideal. Thus
height p1 = 1, and dim A/p1 = n −1 by Theorem 5.12. As (0) ⊊p2/p1 ⊊· · · ⊊pr/p1 is a
maximal chain of prime ideals of A/p1, we have r −1 = n −1. Thus r = n = dim A.
□
This corollary means that A is catenary in the sense that for prime ideals p, q of A with
p ⊆q, every maximal chain of prime ideals between p and q have the same length.
5.4. Dimension of algebraic varieties. Let X be a quasi-projective variety. The di-
mension of X is
dim X := trdegC C(X).
If U ⊆X is an irreducible open subset with U , ∅, then dim U = dim X since C(U) =
C(X). When X is empty, we set dim ∅:= −1. For an irreducible closed subset Y ⊆X,
the codimension of Y in X is
codimX Y := dim X −dim Y.
Corollary 5.14. If X ⊆An is an affine variety and Y = V(p) ⊆X is an irreducible closed
subset for a prime ideal p ⊆A(X), then
dim X = dim A(X) and codimX Y = height p.
If X ⊆PN is a projective variety and Y = V+(p) ⊆X is an irreducible closed subset for a prime
ideal p ⊆S(X), then
dim X = dim S(X) −1 and codimX Y = height p.
Proof. Suppose that X is affine.
As C(X) = F A(X), we have dim X = dim A(X)
by Theorem 5.12. Note that A(Y) = A(X)/p. Then Theorem 5.12 also shows that
codimX Y = height p. Suppose that X is projective. Choose a inear form ℓ∈S(X) with
ℓ, 0. Note that F(S(X)) = C(X)(ℓ) and ℓis transcendental over C(X). By Theorem 5.12,
dim S(X) = trdegC F(S(X)) = trdegC C(X) + 1 = dim X + 1. The remaining is clear.
□

Corollary 5.17. Let X, Y ⊆An be affine varieties or X, Y ⊆PN be projective varieties.
(1) If Z is an irreducible component of V(f1, . . . , fc) for f1, . . . , fc ∈A(X) or S(X), then
dim Z ≥dim X −c.
(2) If Z is an irreducible component of V(f) for f ∈A(X) or S(X) with f , 0, then dim Z =

(3) If Z is an irreducible component of X ∩Y, then dim Z ≥dim X + dim Y −n.

an irreducible component Z of V( f1, . . . , fc) corresponds to a prime p ⊆A(X) minimal

dim Z = dim(A(X)/p) = dim A(X) −height p ≥dim X −c.

For (3), consider the affine variety

If X is a nonempty algebraic set, then we can write X = X1∪· · ·∪Xr, where X1, . . . , Xr
are irreducible components of X. The dimension of X is
dim X := max
1≤i≤r dim Xi.
Corollary 5.18. Let X be an algebraic set. Then dim X is the supremum of the lengths r of
strict chains Y0 ⊊Y1 ⊊· · · ⊊Yr of irreducible closed subsets of X. If X is a quasi-projective
variety, then all maximal strict chains of irreducible closed subsets of X has the same length
dim X.
Proof. Choose a nonempty affine open subset Y of an irreducible component of X
having maximal dimension. Then dim X = dim Y. A chain of prime ideals of A(Y)
corresponds to a chain of irreducible closed subsets of Y of the same length. Taking
the closures of these subsets in X, we obtain a chain of irreducible closed subsets of X
of the same length. Thus the maximal length of a chain of irreducible closed subsets of
X is at least dim X. Conversely, consider a maximal chain of irreducible closed subsets
of X. The smallest member of such a chain must be a point. Choose a nonempty affine
open subset Y of X containing this point. Applying the above argument in the reverse
