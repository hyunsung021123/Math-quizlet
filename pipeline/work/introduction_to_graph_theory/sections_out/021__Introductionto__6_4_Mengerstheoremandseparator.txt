This lemma provides a way to construct every 3-connected graph.

Theorem 6.17. A graph is 3-connected if and only if it can be obtained from K4 by a sequence of
vertex 3-splits.

Proof. Starting from a 3-connected graph, repeatedly contract a 3-contractible edge while
more than four vertices remain. The lemma allows every step, and the ﬁnal graph is K4, the
only 3-connected graph on four vertices. Reversing these contractions gives vertex 3-splits:
each restored vertex has degree at least 3, and the neighborhoods have exactly the union
required in the deﬁnition of a split. Conversely, K4 is 3-connected, and vertex 3-splits preserve
3-connectivity.
⇤

We prove the theorem by ﬁrst allowing the endpoints to range over two vertex sets. The
advantage is that a minimum separator can then divide the problem into two smaller ones.

Deﬁnition 6.23. For X, Y ✓V(G), an X, Y-path meets X only at its ﬁrst vertex and Y only
at its last vertex. In a digraph it is directed from X to Y. A path of length zero at a
vertex of X \ Y is allowed. An X, Y-separator is a set Z ✓V(G) meeting every X, Y-path.
Unlike an x, y-separating set, it may contain endpoints; in particular, both X and Y are
X, Y-separators.

Theorem 6.24(Menger’s theorem, set form). In a graph or digraph G, the minimum size of an
X, Y-separator equals the maximum number of pairwise vertex-disjoint X, Y-paths.

Proof. Every separator meets the disjoint paths in distinct vertices, so only the existence of
enough paths needs proof. We use induction on |V(G)| + |E(G)|. Let k be the minimum size of
an X, Y-separator. For k ⇤0 there is no X, Y-path, and the assertion is immediate. Assume
that k ≥1.

Case 1. There is a minimum X, Y-separator Z diﬀerent from both X and Y. Let G1 be induced
by Z and all vertices on X, Z-paths, and let G2 be induced by Z and all vertices on Z, Y-paths.
These graphs meet only in Z: an intersection outside Z would give a walk from X to Y avoiding

Z, and hence an X, Y-path avoiding Z. Moreover, no vertex of Y \ Z belongs to G1, and no
vertex of X \ Z belongs to G2. Both diﬀerences are nonempty, since |Z| ⇤k min{|X|, |Y|}
and Z , X, Y. Thus both graphs have fewer vertices than G.

Every X, Y-path contains an X, Z-path before its ﬁrst vertex in Z, and a Z, Y-path after its last
vertex in Z. Consequently, any separator between X \ V(G1) and Z in G1 is an X, Y-separator
in G. Its minimum size is therefore k, since Z itself is one such separator. Induction supplies k
disjoint X, Z-paths in G1. Likewise, it supplies k disjoint Z, Y-paths in G2. Each family uses
every vertex of Z exactly once. Join the two paths at each vertex of Z; since the graphs meet
only in Z, this gives k disjoint X, Y-paths.

Case 2. Every minimum X, Y-separator equals X or Y. We may assume that |X| ⇤k; if
necessary, interchange X, Y and, in a digraph, reverse all arcs. If X ✓Y, the k paths of length
zero at the vertices of X suﬃce. Otherwise, choose x 2 X \ Y. Since X \ {x} is not a separator,
there is an x, Y-path avoiding X \ {x}. Its ﬁrst edge or arc is e ⇤xw with w < X.

So far we have connected two speciﬁed vertices by independent paths. It is also useful to
connect one vertex to a set.

Deﬁnition 6.28. For x 2 V(G) and U ✓V(G) \ {x}, an x, U-fan of size k is a family of k
paths from x to U that pairwise meet only at x and meet U only at their other endpoints.

Connectivity guarantees the existence of such fans. We ﬁrst record a simple consequence of
the deﬁnition of k-connectedness; its proof is left as an exercise.

Lemma 6.29(Expansion lemma). If G is a k-connected graph and G0 is formed from G by adding
a vertex y with at least k neighbors in G, then G0 is k-connected.

Lemma 6.30(Fan lemma; Dirac 21071). A graph with more than k vertices is k-connected if and
only if, for every x 2 V(G) and every U ✓V(G) \ {x} with |U| ≥k, it has an x, U-fan of size k.

Proof. Suppose ﬁrst that G is k-connected. Add a new vertex y adjacent to every vertex of
U. By Lemma 6.29 the resulting graph is k-connected, so Theorem 6.26 gives k independent
x, y-paths. Truncate each path at its ﬁrst vertex in U.

Conversely, suppose that G is not k-connected. Choose a separating set S with |S| < k and
vertices x, y in distinct components of G −S. Since |V(G)| > k, we may enlarge S, without
using x or y, so that |S| ⇤k −1. Set U :⇤S [ {y}. An x, U-fan of size k would have to use
every vertex of U as an endpoint. In particular, its path ending at y would have to avoid S
internally, contradicting that every x, y-path meets S.
⇤

6.5Cycles through prescribed vertices and Hamiltonian cycles

Cycles are closely related to several notions introduced above: cut-edges are precisely the
edges lying on no cycle, and minimum degree forces long cycles. We now study further
connections among cycles, connectivity, and minimum degree.
