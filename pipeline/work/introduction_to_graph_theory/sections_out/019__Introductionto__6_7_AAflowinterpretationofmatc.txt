For integral capacities, start with zero ﬂow and repeatedly augment along a residual

Bipartite matching. For an X, Y-bigraph G, direct its edges from X to Y, add arcs sx and

yt, and give these new arcs capacity one. Give the arcs from X to Y capacity |V(G)| + 1. An
integral ﬂow is a sum of unit ﬂows along paths sxyt and hence corresponds to a matching.
A minimum cut has capacity at most |X|, so it contains no arc from X to Y. Its source side S
therefore gives the vertex cover (X \ S) [ (Y \ S), whose size equals its capacity. Conversely,
a cover C gives the cut with source side {s} [ (X \ C) [ (Y \ C) and capacity |C|. The ﬂow
theorem recovers the König–Egerváry equality, and hence Hall’s theorem.

Disjoint paths. Give each arc of a digraph capacity one. Delete arcs entering s or leaving t,
since they lie on no directed s, t-path. An integral ﬂow of value k gives k arc-disjoint s, t-paths:
ﬁrst subtract ﬂow around directed cycles, then follow positive-ﬂow arcs from s to t, subtracting
one unit each time. Conservation prevents a path from stopping at an internal vertex. After
deleting any set of arcs meeting all s, t-paths, the vertices still reachable from s determine a
cut using only deleted arcs. Thus a minimum cut is a smallest such set, recovering the edge

To obtain internally vertex-disjoint paths, suppose that there is no edge or arc from s to t.
Replace each other vertex v by v−, v+ and an arc v−v+ of capacity one. Redirect every original
