Lemma 7.32. If a connected graph G has a nonempty degree-choosable induced subgraph H, then
G is degree-choosable.

Proof. Let L be a list assignment for G with |L(v)| ≥dG(v) for all v. For each component F of
G −V(H), connectedness of G gives a vertex yF 2 V(F) having a neighbor in H. Hence

|L(yF)| ≥dG(yF) > dF(yF).

By Lemma 7.26, each component F has an L|V(F)-coloring. Combining these colorings gives an
L-coloring f of G −V(H).

Now form a list assignment L0 for H by deleting from L(v) the colors used by f on neighbors
of v. We lose at most one color for each neighbor not in V(H). Since |L(v)| ≥dG(v), we have
|L0(v)| ≥dH(v) for v 2 V(H). Since H is degree-choosable, H has an L0-coloring f 0. Together,

f and f 0 form an L-coloring of G.
⇤

This reduces our problem to ﬁnding a degree-choosable induced subgraph. To state the
next lemma we need, we deﬁne a chord of a cycle to be an edge not in the cycle whose endpoints
are in the cycle.

Lemma 7.33. An even cycle with at most one chord is degree-choosable.

Proof. Let H be an even cycle with at most one chord. Then H is 2-connected. Let L be a list
assignment such that |L(v)| ≥dH(v) for all v. If the lists are not identical, then Lemma 7.31
implies that H is L-colorable. If all lists are identical, alternate two colors around the cycle.

Lemma 7.34. In a triangle-free graph with at least one even cycle, the shortest even cycle has at
most one chord.

Proof. Let C be a shortest even cycle in G. A chord of C must join two vertices at even distance
on C; otherwise the chord together with one of the two paths on C would form a shorter even
cycle. Moreover, triangle-freeness implies that each of the two paths between the endpoints of
a chord has length at least 4.

Suppose that C has two chords. If their endpoints do not alternate on C, the two chords
together with the two intervening paths form a shorter even cycle. If their endpoints alternate,
the two cycles formed using both chords are even and their lengths sum to |C| + 4; since
|C| ≥8, one is shorter than C. Both cases contradict the choice of C. Hence C has at most one
chord.
⇤

Lemma 7.35(Erdős–Rubin–Taylor, 210810). Every 2-connected graph G that is not a complete graph
or an odd cycle has an even cycle with at most one chord.

Proof. Let Q be a largest clique in G. Since G is not complete, it has a vertex outside Q. By
the fan lemma (Lemma 6.30), there are two paths from this vertex to distinct vertices of Q
whose interiors avoid Q and which meet only at their starting vertex. Hence we may choose a
shortest path P with distinct endpoints u, v 2 Q and a nonempty set of internal vertices, all
outside Q. Let ` be its length.

If ` ⇤2, write P ⇤uxv. Then u, x, v, y, u forms a 4-cycle with the single chord uv, where
y 2 Q is a nonneighbor of x guaranteed by the maximality of Q. Hence we may assume
that every vertex outside Q has at most one neighbor in Q. Since P has minimum length, its
internal vertices have no neighbors in Q except through the end edges of P.

If ` is odd, then adding uv completes a chordless even cycle with P. If ` is even and there
exists y 2 Q −{u, v}, then adding the path uyv completes an even cycle whose only chord is
uv.

The only remaining case is when G is triangle-free and we have obtained a chordless odd
cycle C. By hypothesis, C , G. Since C has no chord, G has a vertex z outside C. Lemma 6.30
gives two paths from z to distinct vertices a, b 2 V(C), meeting only at z and otherwise
avoiding C. Their union is an a, b-path whose internal vertices lie outside C. Including the
a, b-paths along C, we have three independent a, b-paths in G. Two of these have lengths of
the same parity and form an even cycle. Hence Lemma 7.34 implies that G has an even cycle
with at most one chord.
⇤

These lemmas yield the following theorem.

Theorem 7.36. If a connected graph G is not degree-choosable, then every block of G is a complete
graph or an odd cycle.

or edge is complete, so B is 2-connected. By Lemma 7.35, B has an even cycle C with at most

Proof of Theorem 7.29. Suppose χ`(G) > ∆(G). By Proposition 7.25, G is not (∆(G) −1)-

implies that every block of G is a complete graph or an odd cycle. Since G is regular, G has
only one block: if there were two or more, the cut-vertex of a leaf block B would have larger
degree in G than the other vertices of B, since B is complete or a cycle, hence regular, while
the cut-vertex also has neighbors outside B. Hence G is a complete graph or an odd cycle.

7.10Edge-coloring of graphs

For some problems, the pairwise relationships between objects are the main concern. For
example, let vertices represent people and let an edge join each pair that must meet. If colors
represent time slots, then two meetings involving the same person cannot receive the same
color. This motivates edge-coloring.

Deﬁnition 7.37. A k-edge-coloring of a loopless multigraph G is a labeling of its edges
from a set of k colors; it is proper if incident edges receive distinct colors. We call G
k-edge-colorable if it has a proper k-edge-coloring, and the edge-chromatic number or
chromatic index χ0(G) is the minimum such k.

Every color class of a proper edge-coloring is a matching. A loop cannot belong to a
matching, which is why edge-coloring is restricted to loopless multigraphs. Because parallel
edges must receive distinct colors, edge-coloring behaves diﬀerently for multigraphs and
simple graphs.

For loopless multigraphs, it is not diﬃcult to see that χ0(G) ⇤χ(L(G)), where L(G) is the
line graph of Deﬁnition 6.20.
