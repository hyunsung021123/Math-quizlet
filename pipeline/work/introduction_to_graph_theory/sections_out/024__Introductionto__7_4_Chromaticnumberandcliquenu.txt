The existence of triangle-free graphs with arbitrarily large chromatic number shows that the
chromatic number is not a ‘local’ parameter, in the sense that forbidding small local structures
places little restriction on the chromatic number. Later we will conﬁrm this again by showing
that there are graphs with arbitrarily large girth and arbitrarily large chromatic number. If G
has girth greater than g, then every set of at most g vertices induces a forest, whose chromatic
number is at most two, although the chromatic number of G can be large. Thus the chromatic
number is more of a ‘global’ parameter.

7.5[A] Triangle-free graphs with large chromatic number

The triangle-free k-chromatic graphs produced by Mycielski’s construction have exponentially
many vertices in k. Although forbidding a triangle does not bound the chromatic number, we
can still quantify its eﬀect. How large can the chromatic number of an n-vertex triangle-free
graph be? The following proposition provides an upper bound.
