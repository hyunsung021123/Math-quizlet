Deﬁnition 6.28. For x 2 V(G) and U ✓V(G) \ {x}, an x, U-fan of size k is a family of k
paths from x to U that pairwise meet only at x and meet U only at their other endpoints.

Connectivity guarantees the existence of such fans. We ﬁrst record a simple consequence of
the deﬁnition of k-connectedness; its proof is left as an exercise.

Lemma 6.29(Expansion lemma). If G is a k-connected graph and G0 is formed from G by adding
a vertex y with at least k neighbors in G, then G0 is k-connected.

Lemma 6.30(Fan lemma; Dirac 21071). A graph with more than k vertices is k-connected if and
only if, for every x 2 V(G) and every U ✓V(G) \ {x} with |U| ≥k, it has an x, U-fan of size k.

Proof. Suppose ﬁrst that G is k-connected. Add a new vertex y adjacent to every vertex of
U. By Lemma 6.29 the resulting graph is k-connected, so Theorem 6.26 gives k independent
x, y-paths. Truncate each path at its ﬁrst vertex in U.

Conversely, suppose that G is not k-connected. Choose a separating set S with |S| < k and
vertices x, y in distinct components of G −S. Since |V(G)| > k, we may enlarge S, without
using x or y, so that |S| ⇤k −1. Set U :⇤S [ {y}. An x, U-fan of size k would have to use
every vertex of U as an endpoint. In particular, its path ending at y would have to avoid S
internally, contradicting that every x, y-path meets S.

6.5Cycles through prescribed vertices and Hamiltonian cycles

Cycles are closely related to several notions introduced above: cut-edges are precisely the
edges lying on no cycle, and minimum degree forces long cycles. We now study further
connections among cycles, connectivity, and minimum degree.

A cycle in an n-vertex graph has length at most n. A cycle of length n is spanning, which
motivates the following deﬁnition.

Deﬁnition 6.32. A spanning cycle is called a Hamiltonian cycle. A graph is Hamiltonian if it
contains a Hamiltonian cycle.

The minimum-degree bound of Proposition 3.6 guarantees a cycle of length at least δ(G)+1.
It yields a Hamiltonian cycle when δ(G) ⇤n −1, but this covers only the complete graph. We
ﬁrst record a simple obstruction to Hamiltonicity, which explains why the thresholds below
cannot be lowered.

6.6[A] Closure and Chvátal’s degree-sequence theorem

We now deﬁne the closure operation used to apply Ore’s lemma repeatedly.

Deﬁnition 6.37. A (Hamiltonian) closure of an n-vertex graph G is obtained by repeatedly
joining nonadjacent vertices whose current degree sum is at least n, until no such pair
remains. A graph is closed if no such pair exists.

Theorem 6.38(Bondy–Chvátal, 21087). The closure of a graph is unique; that is, it does not depend
on the order of edge additions. Denote it by C(G). A graph is Hamiltonian if and only if its closure
is Hamiltonian.

Proof. From an n-vertex graph G, form a closure G1 by adding e1, . . . , er in order and a closure
G2 by adding f1, . . . , fs in order. Each addition joins vertices whose degree sum is at least n
at that time. Once a pair becomes addable, it remains addable and therefore must be joined
before the process terminates.

If G2 * G1, let fi be the ﬁrst edge in the second sequence that is absent from G1. Since
f1, . . . , fi−1 2 E(G1), the edge fi is addable to G1 and therefore must also belong to G1, a
contradiction. Hence G2 ✓G1; by symmetry, G1 ✓G2.

The second assertion follows from Lemma 6.36.
⇤
