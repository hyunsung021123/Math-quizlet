7.8List coloring and Brooks’ theorem

Returning to the frequency-assignment problem, some frequency channels might not be
available at a certain location. In other words, when we seek a proper coloring, some colors
may not be available at certain vertices. This leads to the more general concept of list coloring.

Deﬁnition 7.23. For a graph G, a list assignment L is a function on V(G) such that L(v)
is a set of colors ‘acceptable’ for v. An L-coloring is a proper coloring f of G such that

f (v) 2 L(v) for all v. A graph G is k-choosable or list k-colorable if it has an L-coloring
whenever |L(v)| ≥k for all v. The list chromatic number (also called the choice number or
choosability), denoted χ`(G), is the minimum k such that G is k-choosable.

From this deﬁnition, it is easy to see that χ(G) χ`(G) holds. However, the gap between
these two parameters can be large as the list chromatic number behaves quite diﬀerently
from the chromatic number. For example, consider the complete bipartite graph Km,m with

However, some bounds on the chromatic number still hold for the list chromatic number.
For example, the greedy algorithm still works for list coloring and yields the following
proposition.

Proposition 7.25. Every k-degenerate graph is (k + 1)-choosable. Thus χ`(G) ∆(G) + 1.

Thus, we have χ(G) χ`(G) ∆(G) + 1. As we have seen before, this bound is tight for
complete graphs and odd cycles. Brooks’ theorem shows that these are the only connected
graphs with χ(G) ⇤∆(G) + 1. Its short proof uses the following list-coloring lemma, which
improves the greedy bound when one vertex has a spare color.

Lemma 7.26. Let G be a connected graph, and let L be a list assignment such that |L(v)| ≥d(v)
for all vertices v. If |L(y)| > d(y) for some vertex y, then G is L-colorable.

Proof. Root a spanning tree at y and order the vertices by repeatedly deleting a leaf distinct
from y, placing y last. Color in the order in which vertices were deleted. Every vertex other
than y has an uncolored parent when it is colored, so fewer than dG(v) colors are forbidden.
At the last step, |L(y)| > dG(y) guarantees an available color for y.
⇤

A ﬁrst application shows that, unlike complete bipartite graphs, even cycles are as easy to
list-color as to color.

Theorem 7.28(Brooks, 21052). If a connected graph G is neither a complete graph nor an odd cycle,
then χ(G) ∆(G).

Proof (Lovász, 1975). Let ∆⇤∆(G). If ∆2, then G is a path or an even cycle, since K1, K2,
and odd cycles are excluded; hence χ(G) 2 ⇤∆. Assume that ∆≥3, and give every vertex
the list [∆].

If G is not regular, some vertex y has d(y) < ∆⇤|L(y)|, and Lemma 7.26 gives a ∆-coloring
of G.

Suppose that G is regular and has a cut-vertex v. For each component C of G −v, the graph
GC ⇤G[V(C) [ {v}] is connected and dGC(v) < ∆, while every other vertex of GC has degree
at most ∆. Lemma 7.26 gives a ∆-coloring of each GC. After permuting colors, all of them give
v the same color, and together they form a ∆-coloring of G.

Finally, suppose that G is regular and 2-connected. We claim that G has vertices x, y, z
such that xy, xz 2 E(G), yz < E(G), and G −{y, z} is connected. Given the claim, color both

y and z with color 1. On G0 ⇤G −{y, z}, let L0(v) ⇤[∆] \ {1} if v is adjacent to y or z,
and L0(v) ⇤[∆] otherwise. A vertex that loses a color also loses at least one neighbor, so
|L0(v)| ≥dG0(v) for all v 2 V(G0). The vertex x loses two neighbors but only one color, so
|L0(x)| ⇤∆−1 > ∆−2 ⇤dG0(x). Lemma 7.26 gives an L0-coloring of G0, and together with
color 1 on y and z this is a ∆-coloring of G. The point is that x has two neighbors of the same
color.

It remains to prove the claim. If G is 3-connected, note that some vertex x has two nonadjacent
neighbors y, z: otherwise every neighborhood would be a clique, and the connected graph G
would be complete. Then G −{y, z} is connected because G is 3-connected.

Otherwise G is not 3-connected but has at least ∆+ 1 ≥4 vertices, so it has a separating set
of size at most two; by 2-connectivity, this set has the form {x, w}. Then G −x is connected
and w is a cut-vertex of G −x, so the block-cutpoint graph of G −x is a tree with at least
two leaf blocks B1, B2. For i ⇤1, 2, let ci be the cut-vertex of G −x in Bi. The vertex x has a
neighbor yi 2 V(Bi)\ {ci}; otherwise ci would separate Bi −ci from the rest of G, contradicting
2-connectivity. Put y ⇤y1 and z ⇤y2. They are nonadjacent, because each lies in only one
block of G −x. Each Bi −yi is connected and still contains ci. Since yi lies only in the block Bi,
deleting y and z leaves every other block of G −x intact, so G −{x, y, z} is connected. Finally,
d(x) ⇤∆≥3, so x has a neighbor other than y and z, and G −{y, z} is connected.
⇤

The list version of Brooks’ theorem is also true, but the proof above does not carry over: in
the last two cases we permuted colors or gave y and z the same color, which lists need not
allow.

Lemma 7.33. An even cycle with at most one chord is degree-choosable.

Proof. Let H be an even cycle with at most one chord. Then H is 2-connected. Let L be a list

implies that H is L-colorable. If all lists are identical, alternate two colors around the cycle.
