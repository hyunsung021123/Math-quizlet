7.6Chromatic polynomials

The chromatic number asks whether a proper coloring exists. We now count all of them. For a
positive integer k, let PG(k) be the number of proper vertex colorings of G with colors in [k].
The same deﬁnition extends to multigraphs. Parallel edges impose the same inequality twice
and do not aﬀect this number. A loop makes a proper coloring impossible, so PG(k) ⇤0 when
G has a loop.

of C4 −e ⇤P4 in which those endpoints receive the same color become proper colorings

Theorem 7.13(Deletion–contraction). If e is an edge of a graph G, then

PG(k) ⇤PG−e(k) −PG/e(k).

Consequently PG(k) agrees, on positive integers, with a polynomial in k of degree |V(G)| and
leading coeﬃcient one.

Here G/e is a graph: after identifying the endpoints of e and removing e, replace any resulting
parallel edges by a single edge. This does not aﬀect proper vertex colorings.

Proof. Every proper coloring of G −e belongs to exactly one of two classes. If the endpoints of
e have diﬀerent colors, it is a proper coloring of G. If they have the same color, identifying
the endpoints gives a proper coloring of G/e, and this operation is reversible. Thus PG−e(k) ⇤
PG(k) + PG/e(k).

Induct on the number of edges. The edgeless graph on n vertices has PG(k) ⇤kn. Deletion
preserves the number of vertices, while contraction reduces it by one. Both G −e and G/e have
fewer edges than G, so induction applies to both. Hence the recurrence gives a polynomial of
degree n whose leading term comes only from the deletion term and is kn.
⇤

For the Tutte polynomial we will instead retain all edges other than the contracted edge,
including any resulting parallel edges or loops. The deletion–contraction identity above also
holds for a non-loop edge of a multigraph under that convention: parallel edges do not aﬀect
the chromatic polynomial, while a loop makes it zero. The degree and leading-coeﬃcient
conclusion requires the multigraph to be loopless.

7.7[A] Acyclic orientations and broken circuits

This section gives two combinatorial interpretations of the chromatic polynomial beyond
counting colorings: its value at −1 counts acyclic orientations, and its coeﬃcients count edge
sets that contain no broken circuit. The two results meet at the end of the section.

Acyclic orientations

An orientation of G is acyclic if it has no directed cycle. The polynomial also contains information
at values that are not numbers of colors. For example, PC4(−1) ⇤24 −2 ⇤14. Of the 24 ⇤16
orientations of C4, exactly two are directed cycles, leaving fourteen acyclic orientations. This is
an instance of the following theorem.
