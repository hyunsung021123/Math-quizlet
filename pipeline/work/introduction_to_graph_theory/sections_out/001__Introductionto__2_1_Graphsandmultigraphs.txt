2.1Graphs and multigraphs

We begin with a deﬁnition broad enough to model the seven bridges in Figure 1.1.

Deﬁnition 2.1. A multigraph G consists of a ﬁnite vertex set V(G) and a ﬁnite multiset E(G)
whose members are one- or two-element subsets of V(G). A one-element edge is a loop,
and repeated two-element edges are parallel edges. The order and size of G are |G| :⇤|V(G)|
and e(G) :⇤|E(G)|, respectively.

A multigraph is simple if it has no loops or parallel edges. Unless stated otherwise, the
word graph means a ﬁnite simple graph.

We write [n] :⇤{1, 2, . . . , n} and abbreviate the edge {u, v} as uv. For a loop at u we write
uu. For example, the multigraph

G ⇤!

[5], {{11, 12, 12, 23, 34, 45, 15}}"

has order 5 and size 7. The two copies of 12 are distinct edge occurrences.

The following terminology will be used throughout.

• Distinct vertices u and v are adjacent if uv 2 E(G).

• A vertex v and an edge e are incident if v is an endpoint of e. Two distinct edges are
adjacent if they share an endpoint.

• A vertex adjacent to v is a neighbor of v; the set of all such vertices is NG(v), or simply
N(v).

• The degree dG(v) is the number of edge-ends incident with v, counted with multiplicity.
Thus a loop contributes two. For a simple graph, dG(v) ⇤|NG(v)|.

Parallel edges do not change vertex coloring or the existence of a matching, but they do
matter in questions about edge cuts. We will therefore state explicitly whenever multigraphs
are allowed.
