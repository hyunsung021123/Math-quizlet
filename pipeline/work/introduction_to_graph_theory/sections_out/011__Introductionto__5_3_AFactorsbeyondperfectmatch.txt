5.4[A] Algorithmic aspects of matchings

Brute force examines all 2m edge subsets of an m-edge graph. A greedy algorithm is faster
but may stop at a maximal matching that is not maximum: on P6, for example, the second and
fourth edges form a maximal matching of size two, although a matching of size three exists.

Deﬁnition 5.22. Given a matching M, an M-alternating path alternates between edges in M
and edges outside M. An M-augmenting path is a positive-length alternating path whose

Theorem 5.23(Berge, 1957). A matching M in a graph G is maximum if and only if G has no

M-augmenting path.

Proof. An augmenting path immediately produces a larger matching, as observed above.
Conversely, suppose that G has a matching M0 with |M0| > |M|, and consider the spanning
subgraph with edge set M4M0. Every component is a path, an even cycle, or an isolated
vertex, and its edges alternate between M and M0. Since M0 has more edges in total, some
path component has one more edge of M0 than of M. It begins and ends with edges of M0 and
is therefore an M-augmenting path.
⇤
