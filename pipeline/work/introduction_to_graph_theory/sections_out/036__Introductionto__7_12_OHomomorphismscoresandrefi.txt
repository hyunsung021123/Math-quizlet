Corollary 7.58(Perfect Graph Theorem; Lovász, 21083). A graph is perfect if and only if its comple-
ment is perfect.

Proof. Induced subgraphs of G are the complements of induced subgraphs of G, and ↵(H) ⇤
!(H) and !(H) ⇤↵(H). Hence the condition in Theorem 7.57 holds for G if and only if it
holds for G.
⇤

An odd hole is an induced odd cycle of length at least 5, and an odd antihole is the complement
of an odd hole. Berge also conjectured the Strong Perfect Graph Theorem: a graph is perfect if
and only if it contains neither an odd hole nor an odd antihole. Equivalently, the minimally
imperfect graphs are precisely the odd holes and odd antiholes. The self-complementary
formulation implies the Perfect Graph Theorem. Chudnovsky, Robertson, Seymour, and
Thomas announced a proof in 2002; the full paper appeared in 2006 [CRST06].

Deﬁnition 7.60. An endomorphism is a homomorphism G ! G. A graph H is a core if
every endomorphism of H is an automorphism. If H is an induced subgraph of G, a
homomorphism r : G ! H that ﬁxes H pointwise is a retraction.

Example 7.61(The core is the irreducible target). Every connected bipartite graph with an
edge maps to K2, and K2 maps back along any edge. Its core is therefore K2. In contrast, an
odd cycle C2r+1 is a core. Indeed, an endomorphism maps its closed walk of odd length to
an odd closed walk in the same cycle. A proper image would be a path, which is bipartite
and contains no odd closed walk. Hence the image is the entire cycle, and a surjective
endomorphism of a ﬁnite cycle is an automorphism.

Proposition 7.62. Every ﬁnite graph is homomorphically equivalent to a core, unique up to
isomorphism.

Proof. Choose an endomorphism f : G ! G whose vertex image has minimum size, and
let H :⇤G[ f (V(G))]. The choice of f implies that f |H is bĳective; otherwise f 2 would have
a smaller image. A bĳective endomorphism of a ﬁnite graph is an automorphism, so some
positive power of f |H is the identity. The same power of f is therefore a retraction onto H.
Any nonautomorphic endomorphism of H, composed with f , would give an endomorphism
of G with a smaller image. Hence H is a core.

If cores H and H0 admit homomorphisms a : H ! H0 and b : H0 ! H, then ba and ab are
automorphisms. Thus a is bĳective, and a−1 ⇤(ba)−1b is also a homomorphism. Hence a is an
isomorphism.
