5.2Matchings in general graphs

Hall’s condition does not extend directly to general graphs. For example, K2n+1 has the
largest possible neighborhoods but cannot have a perfect matching because its order is odd.
More generally, disjoint unions of odd cliques illustrate the parity obstruction. The following
deﬁnitions capture the relationship between matching number and this obstruction.

Deﬁnition 5.11. For an integer k ≥0, a k-factor in a graph G is a k-regular spanning
subgraph of G. An odd component is a component of odd order. We write o(G) for the
number of odd components of G.

Corollary 5.5 gives a set S ✓X such that ↵0(G) ⇤|X| −|S| + |N(S)|. If we delete T :⇤N(S),
every vertex of S becomes isolated. Thus, when |S| > |T|, every matching leaves at least
|S| −|T| vertices of S uncovered.

The same idea extends to general graphs. For any T ✓V(G), every matching leaves at least
o(G −T) −|T| vertices uncovered (a vacuous bound when this quantity is negative). As in
Hall’s theorem, this turns out to be the correct obstruction. First consider a small example.

5.3Augmenting paths

A matching is maximal if no edge can be added to it, and maximum if it has size ↵0(G). These
notions diﬀer: on P6, the second and fourth edges form a maximal matching of size two,
although a matching of size three exists. The following notion explains how a matching that is
not maximum can be improved.

Deﬁnition 5.19. Given a matching M, an M-alternating path alternates between edges in M
and edges outside M. An M-augmenting path is a positive-length alternating path whose
two endpoints are uncovered by M.

For two edge sets A and B, their symmetric diﬀerence is A4B :⇤(A \ B) [ (B \ A). If P is
M-augmenting, then

M0 :⇤M4E(P) ⇤(M \ E(P)) [ (E(P) \ M)

is a matching with |M0| ⇤|M| + 1. Indeed, the ends of P are uncovered by M, so the ﬁrst and
last edges of P lie outside M, and P has one more edge outside M than in M. An internal
vertex of P is covered by exactly one edge of M, and that edge lies on P; hence in M0 it is
covered only by its other edge on P. The two ends of P are covered only by their edges on P.
