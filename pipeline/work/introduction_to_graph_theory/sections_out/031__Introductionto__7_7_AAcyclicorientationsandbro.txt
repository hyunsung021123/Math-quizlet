7.7[A] Acyclic orientations and broken circuits

This section gives two combinatorial interpretations of the chromatic polynomial beyond
counting colorings: its value at −1 counts acyclic orientations, and its coeﬃcients count edge
sets that contain no broken circuit. The two results meet at the end of the section.

Acyclic orientations

An orientation of G is acyclic if it has no directed cycle. The polynomial also contains information
at values that are not numbers of colors. For example, PC4(−1) ⇤24 −2 ⇤14. Of the 24 ⇤16
orientations of C4, exactly two are directed cycles, leaving fourteen acyclic orientations. This is
an instance of the following theorem.

Broken circuits

The subgraph expansion of Theorem 7.16 has many terms which cancel. We now describe the
edge sets that remain after a systematic cancellation, giving a counting interpretation of every
coeﬃcient.

Deﬁnition 7.20. Fix a total order on E(G). A broken circuit is the edge set of a cycle with its
smallest edge removed. An edge set is NBC (no broken circuit) if it contains no broken
circuit as a subset.

The word “circuit” here refers to a cycle. A broken circuit itself is a path, but not every path is
a broken circuit: the missing edge must belong to G and must be smaller than all edges of the
path.

7.8List coloring and Brooks’ theorem

Returning to the frequency-assignment problem, some frequency channels might not be
available at a certain location. In other words, when we seek a proper coloring, some colors
may not be available at certain vertices. This leads to the more general concept of list coloring.

Deﬁnition 7.23. For a graph G, a list assignment L is a function on V(G) such that L(v)
is a set of colors ‘acceptable’ for v. An L-coloring is a proper coloring f of G such that

f (v) 2 L(v) for all v. A graph G is k-choosable or list k-colorable if it has an L-coloring
whenever |L(v)| ≥k for all v. The list chromatic number (also called the choice number or
choosability), denoted χ`(G), is the minimum k such that G is k-choosable.

From this deﬁnition, it is easy to see that χ(G) χ`(G) holds. However, the gap between
these two parameters can be large as the list chromatic number behaves quite diﬀerently
from the chromatic number. For example, consider the complete bipartite graph Km,m with
